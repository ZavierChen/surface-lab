How to use this demo

Left-drag grabs and rotates the figure in 3D; the near side follows the pointer. Right-drag turns the picture clockwise or counterclockwise about the coordinate origin, like a steering wheel, without changing the viewing direction. Hold both mouse buttons and drag to pan; no Shift key is needed. Scroll to zoom. X, Y and Z view look toward the origin from the positive axis using orthographic projection. Sliders preserve your current view. Reset view returns to the default first-octant perspective.

Choose a preset, then vary coefficients, exponents and Q. A zero coefficient removes that term. Number fields accept finer values than the sliders.

In explicit mode, Q translates the surface along the solved axis. In implicit mode, Q selects a level set and can change the surface type.

Changing one exponent from 2 to 1 does not automatically make a saddle. After solving for the linear variable, same-sign squares give an elliptic paraboloid; opposite-sign squares give a saddle.

Quadrics use analytic geometry. General cubic and quartic surfaces use a 29³, 41³ or 57³ sampling grid. For small features, reduce the range and increase quality. Transparent surfaces may show depth-order artifacts.
